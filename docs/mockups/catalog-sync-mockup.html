SQS ages out a federated search catalog’s messages

A failure scenario for Federated Search for Amazon S3 with a Splunk-native data catalog. The bucket sends each change to the dataset to an SQS queue, and Splunk retrieves the queue to keep the catalog in sync. When events wait longer than the queue’s message retention period, 4 days by default, SQS deletes them unread. The catalog never learns of those changes, and federated searches miss data that is still in the bucket. With it: a path for the sync and the search, two components, seven stages, and a symptom in Diagnose.

    What the scenario rests on

    The catalog
    Splunk software creates and maintains it. Keeping it in sync with the dataset is a choice: a standard SQS queue in your AWS account, sent the bucket’s object create, removal, restore and lifecycle events for the dataset’s prefix.
    The queue
    SQS keeps a message for the queue’s message retention period: 60 seconds to 14 days, 4 days by default. Older messages expire and are deleted. A dead-letter queue takes only messages received too many times.
    Seeing it
    In CloudWatch, ApproximateAgeOfOldestMessage near the retention period, and ApproximateNumberOfMessagesVisible growing.
    Not said
    Splunk’s pages don’t say how fast Splunk retrieves the queue, or how to resync the catalog. The scenario names both causes, more events than are retrieved or retrieval stopping, and sends the resync to Splunk Support.
